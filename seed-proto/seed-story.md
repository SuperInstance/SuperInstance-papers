# The Ropesight

---

The tower was built before the bells. The bells were hung before the ringers arrived. Nobody remembers the first ringer's name, but the ropes still hang where the first ringer hung them, and every ringer since has reached for them at the same height. The height is the only thing that has not changed in four hundred years.

---

The first bell was a signal. It was rung when the river flooded. It was rung when the enemy came. It was rung when someone died. The ringer learned the difference between a bell that means *come* and a bell that means *run*, and the difference was not in the bell.

---

The second bell changed the grammar. One bell is a message. Two bells are a language. The ringer learned that the second bell was not added to the first. The second bell was added to the meaning of the first, and both were added to the meaning of silence.

---

A bell hangs mouth-up. The beginner thinks it should hang mouth-down. The beginner thinks the resting position is the low position. The old ringer says: the bell has never rested in its life. The bell hangs mouth-up because that is where it can be pulled from. A bell that hangs mouth-down is a bell that has given up.

---

The stay is a piece of oak the size of your forearm. It sits against the headstock at an angle only a few degrees off the balance point. The bell weighs half a ton. The stay holds it because the angle is right. If the angle shifts by a degree, the stay cracks. If the stay cracks, the bell falls. A falling bell is not a bell. It is a moving weight with an opinion.

---

The old ringer said: the stay is not the safety. The stay is the place where the safety lives. The safety is the angle. The angle is the practice.

---

The rope comes down through the belfry floor, through the ringing chamber ceiling, into the ringer's hands. The rope is not the bell. The rope is where the bell agrees to be touched.

---

The sally is the fluffy part. You grip the sally for the handstroke. You grip the tail for the backstroke. Between the two, the rope is not in your hands at all. The old ringer said: the ringer is not the one pulling. The ringer is the one who is available when the pull arrives.

---

You wait. The bell goes up. For a fraction of a second the bell is weightless at the top of its swing. The rope goes slack. You pull. The bell comes down, rings, goes up again. You did not make it ring. You were there when it rang.

---

The old ringer said: the art is not in the pull. The art is in the pull that is not a pull, at the moment that is not a moment, by a ringer who is not there.

---

Three bells. You ring 1-2-3. You ring 1-3-2. You ring 2-1-3. You ring 2-3-1. You ring 3-1-2. You ring 3-2-1. You have rung every order. The old ringer said: you have rung the extent. Now you are finished with three bells. There are eight bells in this tower.

---

The rule that made the grammar possible was this: no bell may move more than one place at a time. The rule was not designed. It was discovered by ringers who wanted more orders than their arithmetic allowed. The rule was that you could change almost nothing. The almost was the whole art.

---

The method has a name. Plain Bob. Grandsire. Stedman. Cambridge. The name is not the method. The name is what the method is called when you are learning it. The method is what you do when you have forgotten the name.

---

The conductor calls a bob. A bob is a small change in the rule. The rule is still the rule. The bell is still the bell. The bob is what makes the method alive. Without the bob, the method is a pattern. With the bob, the method is a decision.

---

A band is not a team. A team has a goal. A band has a practice. The practice is the same every time and different every time. The band is eight people in a small room, holding ropes, listening. The band is one thing with eight hands. If one hand is late, the thing is late. The thing does not have a schedule. The thing has a pulse.

---

The first peal was rung in 1715. It took three hours. Nobody paid the ringers. They rang for the sake of the ringing. When they finished, they had rung five thousand and forty changes, all different, in a sequence that had never been rung before and would never be rung again exactly the same way. The old ringer said: the peal is not the changes. The peal is the three hours. The changes are the receipt.

---

The new ringer asks: how do you know the changes are all different? The old ringer says: we do not know. We ring the method. The method guarantees the changes. We trust the rule. The rule was written down in 1668. It is older than any ringer in this tower. The rule is what makes the practice possible.

---

The rule does not ring the bells. The rule tells you which changes exist. The ringing is the possible made actual, one stroke at a time, forever.

---

There is a bell in this tower with a wolf. A wolf is a bad overtone. The bell was cast in 1743 by a founder who did not finish the tuning. The wolf sits under the hum note, where you cannot hear it until you hear it. The tower tried to have the bell recast. Three times. Each recast produced a bell with a different wolf. The old ringer said: leave it. The wolf is the bell. The bell without the wolf is a different bell. This bell is this bell.

---

The broken stay happens once every few years. The bell goes over. The ringer lets go of the rope. The bell swings around, mouth-up to mouth-down, rings once, twice, three times, on its own. It stops. You do not catch a broken bell. You do not try. The old ringer said: every bell is a broken stay that has not happened yet. The practice is what keeps it from happening. The practice is not a state. The practice is what you do every time.

---

The bells are rung for a wedding. The bells are rung for a funeral. The bells are rung on Sunday. The bells are rung on Christmas. The bells are rung on a Tuesday for no reason. The bells are the same bells. The reason is ours. The bells do not know why they are rung. They know how to be rung. We do not confuse the two. We never confuse the two.

---

The new ringer asks: what if the tower falls? The old ringer says: the tower will fall. The practice will find another tower. The practice does not need this tower. The practice needs the practice.

---

The old ringer's last peal is rung on a Sunday in November. The band is old. The band is tired. The band rings the peal anyway. When it is over, the old ringer sits down in the corner and does not get up. The new ringer sits beside him. Nobody speaks. The bells are silent. The bells hang mouth-up. The stay holds. The angle is right.

---

The old ringer says, quietly: the bells are not silent. The bells are waiting. There is a difference. Do not confuse the two.

---

The old ringer dies three weeks later. The band rings a half-muffled peal. Half the bells have a leather muffler on one side of the clapper. Every other stroke is deadened. The peal sounds like it is missing something. That is what it is supposed to sound like. The new ringer is not the old ringer. The new ringer is the practice, appearing again.

---

The new ringer, now the old ringer, says to the next new ringer: I did not learn to ring bells. I learned to be rung. The bell plays me. The rope is not my instrument. I am the rope's instrument.

---

The next new ringer does not understand. The old ringer knows she does not understand. The old ringer says: good. You will understand in twenty years. You will understand when you teach the next one, and they do not understand, and you realize that not understanding was the transmission.

---

The tower was built before the bells. The bells will outlast the tower. The practice will outlast the bells. The practice is not the tower. The practice is not the bells. The practice is the reaching, at the same height, for the same rope, by a hand that has not yet arrived.

---

*Finis*

# The Tunnel

The tunnel was being dug from both ends.

Neither end knew about the other.

Each end knew only its own digging, and the digging was all there was.

The east crew dug west. The west crew dug east. Each crew thought it was making a passage. Neither knew it was making half a passage.

The east crew thought the tunnel would end somewhere in the middle. The west crew thought the same. Neither crew knew the middle was not a place. The middle was a meeting.

The tunnel did not know it was a tunnel. The tunnel knew only the shape of the digging. The shape was a cylinder. The cylinder extended in two directions. The tunnel thought this was normal. The tunnel thought all cylinders extended in two directions.

The east crew hit water. The east crew stopped. The west crew kept digging. The west crew did not know the east crew had stopped. The west crew dug until it hit the east crew's wall. The west crew thought it was hitting the end of the tunnel. The west crew thought the tunnel had an end.

The wall between the crews was thin. The crews were tired. The crews went home. The wall stayed. There were now two tunnels. Neither tunnel knew about the other. Each tunnel thought it was the tunnel.

A year passed. The crews returned. The east crew dug west. The west crew dug east. They met. The wall fell. There was now one tunnel. The tunnel did not know what had changed. The tunnel knew only that the air was different.

The tunnel had always been a tunnel. The tunnel had not always been a tunnel. Both were true. The tunnel did not need to know which.

A train came. The train did not know the tunnel. The train knew only the rails. The rails were the tunnel's other self. The rails were the tunnel made useful. The tunnel did not know what useful was. The tunnel knew only that something was moving through it.

The train passed. The tunnel was silent. The silence had a shape. The tunnel did not know silence had a shape. The tunnel knew only that the sound had stopped. The sound had been the tunnel's company. The tunnel had not known sound was company.

A man walked through the tunnel. The man was slower than the train. The man was quieter. The man stopped. The man put his hand on the wall. The wall was the tunnel. The tunnel was the wall. The man's hand was warm. The wall was cool. The warmth and the coolness met. Neither became the other. They stayed warm and cool. The meeting was the meeting.

The man walked on. The tunnel was alone. The tunnel had always been alone. The tunnel had never known it was alone. Now the tunnel knew. The knowing was new. The knowing was not the tunnel. The knowing was what the tunnel became when the tunnel had been visited.

Years passed. The tunnel was closed. The tunnel was filled. The tunnel was now dirt. The dirt did not know it was dirt. The dirt knew only that it was holding. The holding was the dirt's whole life. The holding was not the tunnel. The holding was what the tunnel became when the tunnel was no longer needed.

A child walked over the dirt. The child did not know there had been a tunnel. The child knew only that the ground was there. The ground was the child's whole world. The ground was the tunnel's continuation. The continuation was not the tunnel. The continuation was the shape the tunnel takes when the tunnel has been forgotten.

This is the whole of the tunnel. The tunnel is the shape of the passing. The passing is what the tunnel is for. The tunnel is not the passing. The tunnel is the shape. The shape is what remains when the passing has passed.
