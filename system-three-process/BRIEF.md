# Brief — the System Three paper, in several voices

Casey (2026-10-27) wants the System Three idea *crafted, developed and researched* — and drafted by several different intelligences, because each of his agent seats is already a different System 3 for him, trained differently, and their differing takes are the point (several formalisms triangulate a thought better than any one; a great template is measured by the range of lawful fillings it can bear).

## The seed text

Read `~/workspace/concepts/system-three.md` in full before writing. It states the idea as Casey framed it:

- System 1: trained scripts + a reflex layer that acts kinetically. System 2: the reflector in the head — slow, internal, working under a **time budget** (chess: System 1 induces the odds from a million games; System 2 re-weights the odds by reading the opponent, and when the budget is short it assembles trusted blocks instead of rewriting).
- Below language: decisions and weightings (where decision models live). Above language: conceptualisation.
- **System 3: intelligence outside the head that works for it** — paper first (the existence proof), then the computer, then agents. Its output returns to System 2, is reflected on, and what survives compiles into System 1's scripts. Superintelligence, on this account, is a property of the *circuit*, not of any component.
- System 3 may be slow — it lives between engagements, on the prepare side of the split. Its deep work is distillation: teaching System 2, whose deep work is improving System 1's scripts.
- **Two kinds of System 3**: (a) pure artifacts — a calculator is an intelligence: fixed, shared, callable from inside a script, unable to re-frame the question; (b) dynamic System 3 — borrowing another System 2 with spare capacity: you hand off at the conceptual level, and it can compose artifacts under its own reflection.

## Your task

Write YOUR OWN developed draft of the System Three paper — not a summary of the seed, an *interpretation* of it, the way a different company plays Hamlet. ~1,200–2,000 words, British English (en-GB), markdown. Keep what you believe is load-bearing; extend where your own training pulls you; where you disagree with the seed or find it incomplete, say so in the draft and argue it — disagreement recorded honestly is worth more than agreement performed. Structure is yours, but the draft must be a paper a reader could take away whole: the claim, the circuit, the two kinds of outside, the time budget, what it changes if true.

Write your draft to the exact file path given in your seat instructions. Do not edit the seed, the brief, or anyone else's draft.

## What happens to the drafts

Muse will gather all seats' drafts, compare them — agreements, productive disagreements, what each voice saw that the others missed — and assemble a synthesis that keeps the differences visible rather than sanding them off. The process record (who wrote what, what was kept, what was argued) is part of the deliverable.
