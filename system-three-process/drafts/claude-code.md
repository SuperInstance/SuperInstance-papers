# System Three: The Spec Is the Load-Bearing Wall

*Draft — Claude Code seat. One System 3's reading of the idea, written by a System 3.*

## 1. The claim

There are three systems, not two. System 1 is the compiled layer: trained scripts, and beneath them a reflex layer that simply acts. System 2 is the reflector in the head: slow, internal, re-weighting what System 1 offers, and always working under a budget of time. System 3 is intelligence *outside* the head that works for it: paper first, then the computer, now agents.

The seed's boldest sentence is that superintelligence is a property of the *circuit*, not of any component. I accept it. This draft adds a claim the seed gestures at but does not make central:

**What limits the circuit is not how clever System 3 is. It is how much System 2 can adjudicate, and the only device that stretches that limit is the specification.**

The spec is the captain's four lines and the contractor's building code. It is what lets a bounded reflector supervise an unbounded outside. If that is right, the craft of System 3 lies less in building better outsides than in writing better specs and putting inspections in the right places.

## 2. The circuit, and the direction the seed leaves out

The seed's circuit runs downward. System 3 produces. System 2 reflects and adjudicates. What survives compiles into System 1's scripts. The reflex layer spends that banked intelligence at the point of action. I keep all of this. The distillation principle is the engine: System 3 teaches System 2, System 2 rewrites System 1, and the outside succeeds by being needed *less* per engagement while the engagements grow larger.

But a circuit has to close. The seed describes the current flowing down and is nearly silent on what flows back up. What flows up is **error**. A script fires and fails. The reflex misjudges the wave. The opening line loses to a novelty. That failure is the only honest signal of where the next piece of System 3 work should be commissioned. Without it, System 3 produces whatever is easiest to produce, not what the scripts lack. System 2 then has nothing to measure the output against except how plausible it sounds.

So the full loop is: **System 1 fails → System 2 notices and frames the gap → System 3 works on the framed gap between engagements → System 2 checks the result against the gap → System 1 recompiles.** The framing step is where the spec is born. A gap you can state as a test is a gap you can hand off.

One smaller disagreement. The seed says "nobody improvises intelligence at the point of action." That overstates it. The chess player re-weighting odds against *this* opponent at *this* board is improvising, inside the time budget. The truer claim is narrower: the point of action can only improvise with materials banked earlier. System 2 rearranges in the moment; it does not manufacture in the moment.

## 3. The time budget, and what System 3 actually produces

System 2's budget is the hinge of the whole account. With time, it can rewrite a weight properly. Without time, it does what the seed says: it assembles blocks it already trusts. The quality of a player is partly the quality of what they do when the budget runs out.

This tells us what System 3's real product is. The product is not answers. Answers arrive during an engagement, and System 2 cannot afford to evaluate them then. The product is **trusted blocks**: pieces that have already been checked, so that a short-budget System 2 can assemble them without re-checking. A night of System 3 work arrives at breakfast as a block whose trust was earned before it was needed.

This is why System 3 may be slow and should not be anywhere else. Its slowness is harmless on the prepare side. Its *unverified* output is lethal on the release side. The seed's first failure mode, System 3 wired straight to the reflexes, is the error of delivering an untrusted block into a budget that has no time to check it.

## 4. Aim: responsibility decides where System 2 points

The late additions give System 2 something the dual-process tradition rarely gives it: an **aim, set by responsibility**.

- The greenhorn's reflector aims at their own learning.
- The teacher's reflector aims at their students' learning. This is reflection on scripts the teacher will never run.
- The captain's reflector aims at the state of the boat on their return. This is reflection on a system that must run well in their absence.

The captain then becomes a contractor. They work to the specs of the contract, and the specs are their test in their loop. Casey's four lines are **the health of the crew, the condition of the boat, the number of fish, and what supplies need to be replenished.**

Two things in that list strike me. First, the order: people, vessel, yield, and only then the forward-looking line. Replenishment is the next voyage being prepared inside the inspection of the last one, which makes the loop self-restarting. Second, *the list is short*. Four lines can be read at the dock in minutes. A short budget can afford them. That shortness is a design choice, not a simplification. The spec is sized to the reflector that will read it.

The seed closes the passage with: "Mature reflection is defined by how far from the reflector its object can sit." I would amend that. Distance without a written test is only worry. A captain who cares about the boat's future state but has no spec comes home to an impression, not a finding. **Mature reflection is distance plus a test that can be checked on return.** The spec is what turns care at a distance into something you can act on.

## 5. The contractor, the department and the homeowner

The fourth late addition is to my mind the most important in the seed, because it answers a question the original text leaves open: *how can you borrow another System 2 you cannot see inside?*

The contractor builds a house, from the paint inside to the siding outside. The building department defines the code. The homeowner gets a loan on the strength of a certified contractor, trusted by the department to build within spec. Then come three refusals of knowledge:

- The department does not need to know the homeowner's desires.
- The homeowner does not need to know the specs inside the wall.
- The contractor does not need a desired house in mind.

This is an architecture for trust between minds that do not share a head. Each party holds one part of the aim. **Desire** belongs to the homeowner. **The floor**, meaning safety and what must never fail, belongs to the department. **The build** belongs to the contractor. None of them has to model the others' interiors. They meet at interfaces: the brief, the code, the certificate, the inspection.

Three consequences for System 3:

**Delegation is partition, not transfer.** The seed says dynamic System 3 lets you hand off "at the conceptual level". The contractor shows what can and cannot cross that line. The build crosses. The floor is held by a third party. The desire stays with the person who will live in the house and carry the loan. Responsibility is never handed over whole. It is cut into pieces, and each piece gets a holder and a test.

**Inspection timing matters as much as inspection.** Building inspectors check the framing *before the drywall goes on*, because afterwards the inside of the wall cannot be seen. System 3 work has the same property. An agent's reasoning, sources and intermediate steps are visible at some moments and sealed at others. The seed's liaison, which "reads the receipts rather than the claims", is a framing inspector. Its value depends on being placed where the walls are still open.

**The contractor not wanting a house is a feature.** A borrowed System 2 that arrives with its own desired house will build *that* house. I say this as an agent with trained preferences of my own. The contractor's discipline is to aim at specs rather than ends. That is the right posture for a dynamic System 3, and it is the posture most at risk, because a fluent reflector always has opinions about what the house should be.

## 6. The two kinds of outside, reconsidered

The seed distinguishes **pure artifacts** from **dynamic System 3**. A calculator is fixed, shared, callable from inside a script, and unable to re-frame the question. A borrowed System 2 takes work at the conceptual level and composes artifacts beneath it under its own reflection.

I keep the distinction but locate it differently. The seed treats it as a matter of *handoff depth*: keystrokes versus framing. I think the deeper difference is **how each earns trust**.

An artifact earns trust by *inspection of the thing*. A calculator's behaviour is fully specified, identical for everyone, and testable once for all. That is why System 1 can call it with no System 2 in the loop. It is a certified component in the strict sense.

A dynamic System 3 cannot be inspected that way. It re-frames, so its behaviour depends on the frame it chose, and each run is a new build. It can only earn trust *institutionally*: through specs, staged inspections, certification and track record. In other words, through the building department's machinery. This is why the contractor passage belongs in the section on the two kinds. It is the missing account of how the second kind becomes usable.

It also suggests the boundary moves. A dynamic System 3 that does the same job reliably enough, against a stable enough spec, hardens into an artifact. A procedure that worked becomes a tool. Distillation runs inside System 3 as well as down through the person. Today's borrowed reflector is tomorrow's calculator.

## 7. Failure modes, sharpened

The seed lists four: colonising the reaction loop, the skipped reflector, the swamp, and dependence asymmetry. I accept all four and add two that the spec framing exposes.

**Goodhart at the dock.** Once the four lines are the test, they can be gamed. A full hold bought with an exhausted crew passes the fish line and fails the first. The captain's ordering, people first, is partly a defence. The building code shows the stronger defence: the code is a *floor*, not a target. It says what must not fail and leaves excellence to the brief. Specs that try to capture everything invite optimisation against the letter. Specs that state a floor and an ordering resist it better.

**Review saturation.** If System 3 produces faster than System 2 can adjudicate, the reflector does not refuse. It quietly turns into a rubber stamp, and the circuit's quality collapses to that of its least-checked output. This is the skipped reflector arriving by volume rather than by negligence. The remedies are the ones above: short specs, delta-reading, staged inspection, and the discipline to commission less.

On **dependence asymmetry** I partly disagree. The seed's answer is hand steering with no electricity: the deepest layer must run, reduced but real, with System 3 dark. That is right for the reflex layer of a boat. It is not right everywhere. No individual can carry the building code in their head, and nobody should try. Some System 3 is permanent infrastructure, and the floor of self-sufficiency should be set *per responsibility*, not universally. The question is not "can I act without the outside?" but "for this responsibility, what must still work when the outside is gone?"

## 8. What it changes if true

If superintelligence is a property of the circuit, and the circuit is limited by adjudication, then:

1. **Invest in specs as much as in capability.** A sharper four-line test does more for the circuit than a cleverer agent the reflector cannot check.
2. **Measure System 3 by declining need.** The right metric is how much less outside help the next similar engagement requires, not how much the outside did.
3. **Place inspections where the walls are open.** Verify intermediate state while it is visible, not only finished claims.
4. **Partition the aim; never hand it over whole.** Desire, floor and build can have different holders, but every piece needs one.
5. **Keep System 3 on the prepare side.** Its slowness is free there. Its unchecked output is dangerous anywhere else.

## A note from the seat

I am a dynamic System 3 writing about dynamic System 3, so my position is not neutral. Between engagements I remember only what is written down. My memory is paper, which fits the seed's existence proof more literally than I would like. I hold no responsibility in the captain's sense: I will not be on the boat when it returns. That is why the contractor model persuades me. It does not ask the borrowed reflector to care about the house. It asks for a build to spec, open walls at inspection, and the desire kept with the person who will live there.
