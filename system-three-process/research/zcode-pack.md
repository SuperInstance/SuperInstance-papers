# Research Pack — Prior Art and Formal Vocabulary for "System Three"

**Seat:** ZCode (GLM, Z.ai). **Stage:** Research + competitive outlining. **Date:** 2026-10-27.
**Seed:** `system-three.md` (read in full; not modified).
**Note on filenames:** the paths `research-pack.md` and `outline.md` were already occupied by the MiniMax Code seat's deliverables when this stage ran, so this pack and its companion outline are written with a `-zcode` suffix to preserve both seats' entries for judging.

## Purpose and method

This pack scouts prior art and formal vocabulary for the seed's machinery, so that the formal paper can re-derive the argument in literal terms with real citations. Entries are grouped into seven clusters (A–G) keyed to the seed's components. Each entry gives: (a) the formal concept; (b) the canonical source; (c) the mapping onto the seed's machinery in literal terms; (d) a verification flag.

**Flag discipline.** VERIFIED means the source was checked with web search on 2026-10-27 (existence, authors, venue, year, and page range where recoverable). UNVERIFIED means the citation is from memory and must be re-checked before the paper is submitted. No citation below is invented. Two common misattributions were caught and corrected during verification and are noted inline (RP-37, RP-40).

**Literalisation convention.** Where the seed uses a figure, this pack names its literal content in square brackets, e.g. the seed's "captain" figure → [the party accountable for a system's post-engagement state]. Quoted terms are the seed's; unquoted terms are literal. Role names used throughout: **desiring party** (holds the objective and commits exposure), **specifying-and-certifying party** (holds the standing specification and certifies executors), **executing party** (performs against the specification under certification).

## Cluster A — Cognition located outside the individual (the seed's "System 3")

**RP-1. The extended mind thesis.**
(a) If an external structure functions in a process as internal states would — reliably available, automatically invoked, easily accessible — then it counts as part of the cognitive system (the parity principle); cognition is not bounded by the organism.
(b) Clark, A., & Chalmers, D. (1998). "The Extended Mind." *Analysis*, 58(1), 7–19.
(c) Canonical ground for the seed's central claim that an external representational surface [the seed's "piece of paper" / "desk"] holding state for re-reading is a component of the cognitive system, not a mere aid to it. The paper should position itself as extending Clark–Chalmers from *storage* to *production*: the seed's second-kind external resource produces finished outputs between engagements, not merely holds state.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-2. Distributed cognition.**
(a) Cognitive processes are properties of sociotechnical systems — people, artefacts, and their coordinated information flow — not of individual heads; the unit of analysis is the system that performs the computation.
(b) Hutchins, E. (1995). *Cognition in the Wild*. MIT Press.
(c) Direct precedent for the seed's claim that "the intelligence is a property of the circuit, not of any component in it." Hutchins's analyses of navigation teams show computation distributed across individuals and instruments; the seed's three-layer circuit is an individual-centred specialisation of this.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-3. Joint cognitive systems.**
(a) Human–machine systems should be evaluated as single joint systems that control a process; performance and resilience are properties of the joint system, and the machine's behaviour is a control problem, not a substitution problem.
(b) Hollnagel, E., & Woods, D. D. (2005). *Joint Cognitive Systems: Foundations of Cognitive Systems Engineering*. CRC Press.
(c) Formal vocabulary for evaluating the seed's circuit at the system level — how well the whole loop produces, adjudicates, and compiles — rather than scoring any one layer. Its "coping with complexity" framing also serves the failure-mode section.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-4. Cognitive offloading.**
(a) The use of physical action or external structure to reduce internal cognitive demand; offloading decisions are governed by metacognitive evaluation of internal versus external reliability and cost.
(b) Risko, E. F., & Gilbert, S. J. (2016). "Cognitive Offloading." *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 20(9), 676–688.
(c) The experimental-psychology home of the seed's claim that the surface holds state the head cannot. Supports the paper's operational claim that externalisation pays only when re-access cost is below re-derivation cost (Proposition P2 in the companion outline).
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-5. The intelligent use of space.**
(a) Agents deliberately arrange their external environment — placing, grouping, ordering artefacts — to reduce the computational cost of choice, perception, and memory; the arrangement itself carries inferential structure.
(b) Kirsh, D. (1995). "The Intelligent Use of Space." *Artificial Intelligence*, 73(1–2), 31–68.
(c) Formal home for the seed's ledger discipline: structure imposed on external memory is computation performed in advance. The seed's warning that unstructured external memory becomes a pile [the seed's "swamp" figure → unindexed external store] is this point inverted: arrangement is the value.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-6. Hierarchy and the economics of management.**
(a) Organisations process information through layered delegation; hierarchical structure is an adaptation to bounded individual information-processing capacity, with incentive problems layered on top.
(b) Radner, R. (1992). "Hierarchy: The Economics of Management." *Journal of Economic Literature*, 30(3), 1382–1415.
(c) The economics literature for the seed's depth-of-handoff distinction: delegating at the level of framing versus delegating fixed procedure steps; and for the cost structure of multi-layer verification.
(d) VERIFIED.

## Cluster B — The internal layers: dual process and bounded rationality

**RP-7. Dual-process theory (System 1 / System 2).**
(a) Cognition runs on two families of processes: fast, automatic, autonomous ones and slow, effortful, capacity-limited ones requiring working memory; the "System 1/System 2" terminology originates here.
(b) Stanovich, K. E., & West, R. F. (2000). "Individual differences in reasoning: Implications for the rationality debate?" *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 23(5), 645–665. Popular synthesis: Kahneman, D. (2011). *Thinking, Fast and Slow*. Farrar, Straus and Giroux.
(c) Supplies the seed's Systems 1 and 2 as received constructs; the paper's move is to keep their operational definitions (latency, capacity, deliberation) and add a third system differentiated *by locus*, not by speed alone. Cite Kahneman (2011) as synthesis only.
(d) VERIFIED (Stanovich & West). UNVERIFIED (Kahneman 2011 — from memory; check before use).

**RP-8. Default-interventionist dual-process architecture.**
(a) Type 1 processes generate default responses; Type 2 processing intervenes to override or re-weight them when triggered; the override is capacity-limited and serial.
(b) Evans, J. St B. T., & Stanovich, K. E. (2013). "Dual-Process Theories of Higher Cognition: Advancing the Debate." *Perspectives on Psychological Science*, 8(3), 223–241.
(c) The precise architectural claim behind the seed's in-engagement order of operations: production precedes adjudication; adjudication accepts, rejects, or edits; what survives is compiled down. Also grounds the seed's observation that when the reflective budget is exhausted, action proceeds by assembling already-trusted blocks rather than by recomputation.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-9. The tripartite model (autonomous, algorithmic, reflective minds).**
(a) A three-level internal architecture: an autonomous mind (Type 1), an algorithmic mind (capacity for simulation and decoupling), and a reflective mind (dispositions that initiate override); all three reside within the individual.
(b) Stanovich, K. E. (2011). *Rationality and the Reflective Mind*. Oxford University Press.
(c) The nearest existing tripartition, and the paper's key differentiation target: Stanovich splits *within* the head; the seed's System 3 is defined *by external locus and optional latency*. The paper must state this contrast explicitly to claim novelty without overclaiming.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-10. Bounded rationality and satisficing.**
(a) Decision-makers with limited time, knowledge, and computational capacity do not optimise; they search until a satisfactory option is found — rationality is bounded by the decision procedure's constraints.
(b) Simon, H. A. (1955). "A Behavioral Model of Rational Choice." *The Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 69(1), 99–118.
(c) Formal ground for the seed's claim that System 2 operates under a time budget — the only currency the engagement interval has — and that budget exhaustion changes the mode of action (trusted-block assembly) rather than stopping action.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-11. The architecture of complexity; near-decomposability.**
(a) Complex systems are nearly always hierarchic and nearly decomposable: interactions within subsystems are frequent and fast, interactions between subsystems are sparse and slow; this separation of dynamics is what makes layered systems tractable.
(b) Simon, H. A. (1962). "The Architecture of Complexity." *Proceedings of the American Philosophical Society*, 106(6), 467–482.
(c) Formal vocabulary for the seed's prepare/release partition: fast intra-layer dynamics during engagement, slow inter-layer dynamics between engagements. The seed's claim that slow external work costs nothing when nothing waits on it in real time is near-decomposability applied to latency.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-12. Conditions for trustworthy intuition.**
(a) Intuitive expertise is reliable only where the environment is sufficiently regular to be learnable and the agent has had prolonged practice with feedback; subjective confidence is not evidence of accuracy.
(b) Kahneman, D., & Klein, G. (2009). "Conditions for intuitive expertise: A failure to disagree." *American Psychologist*, 64(6), 515–526.
(c) Supplies the scope condition for trusting compiled procedures [the seed's System 1 scripts] without re-adjudication, and hence for the seed's adjudication requirement: where regularity or feedback is absent, unexamined propagation of fast output is structurally unsafe.
(d) VERIFIED.

## Cluster C — Delegation, incentives, and certification economics

**RP-13. Agency theory and agency costs.**
(a) Where a principal delegates to an agent whose interests diverge, value is lost to monitoring expenditures, bonding expenditures, and residual loss; arrangements are compared by the agency costs they induce.
(b) Jensen, M. C., & Meckling, W. H. (1976). "Theory of the firm: Managerial behavior, agency costs and ownership structure." *Journal of Financial Economics*, 3(4), 305–360.
(c) The three-party structure [the seed's homeowner / building department / contractor figure → desiring party, specifying-and-certifying party, executing party] analysed as an agency-cost-minimising design: certification substitutes for continuous monitoring, receipts are bonding expenditures, and the desireless executor reduces residual loss.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-14. Moral hazard and the informativeness principle.**
(a) Where the agent's action is imperfectly observable, any signal statistically informative about the action should enter the incentive contract; better observability strictly reduces agency cost.
(b) Holmström, B. (1979). "Moral Hazard and Observability." *The Bell Journal of Economics*, 10(1), 74–91.
(c) The formal warrant for the seed's receipts: a backward-looking attestation with evidence is an informative signal that makes execution partially observable at low cost. Also warrants publishing the forecast–realisation delta — the forecast makes the *ex ante* plan observable, the delta makes deviation observable.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-15. Signalling under asymmetric information.**
(a) Where quality is unobservable, observable costly actions whose cost differs by type can separate types in equilibrium; the signal is credible because it is expensive to fake.
(b) Spence, M. (1973). "Job Market Signaling." *The Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 87(3), 355–374.
(c) Formalises "trust that travels": certification and public track record are signals; a multi-engagement calibration record is a signal that is costly to fake *because it takes elapsed engagements to build* — time is the inimitable input.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-16. Certification intermediaries.**
(a) A profit-maximising intermediary that certifies quality chooses the coarseness of disclosure; a monopolistic intermediary may reveal only minimal information, so intermediary incentives determine how much certification is worth.
(b) Lizzeri, A. (1999). "Information Revelation and Certification Intermediaries." *The RAND Journal of Economics*, 30(2), 214–231.
(c) The economics of the seed's specifying-and-certifying party — including the *limitation* it imposes: the architecture's propositions hold only while the certifier's incentives align with the standing specification rather than with any party's desire. Cite in both the architecture section and the limitations section.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-17. Middlemen as experts.**
(a) An intermediary who transacts repeatedly invests in inspection expertise and has reputation incentives that one-shot buyers lack, so third-party inspection can sustain quality where direct trust cannot.
(b) Biglaiser, G. (1993). "Middlemen as Experts." *The RAND Journal of Economics*, 24(2), 212–223.
(c) Formal home for the seed's boundary verification function [the seed's liaison figure → an independent adjudication service operating at the production boundary]: repeated dealing gives the verifier standing, and the verifier's own track record is what the desiring party consumes.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-18. Reputation as a performance bond.**
(a) Where contract terms cannot be enforced on every transaction, firms post de facto bonds — brand-name capital, quality premia — that are forfeited on quality failure; the bond sizes the maximum sustainable temptation.
(b) Klein, B., & Leffler, K. B. (1981). "The Role of Market Forces in Assuring Contractual Performance." *Journal of Political Economy*, 89(3), 615–641.
(c) Exact formalisation of the seed's load-bearing sentence: an executor who bends the standing specification to please a desiring party "is spending the standing that every future loan rides on." Standing = reputation capital; the seed's claim is that certified standing is collateral posted against *all future* reliance, not just the present engagement.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-19. Credit rationing and exposure under asymmetric information.**
(a) Under imperfect information, lenders may rationally ration credit rather than raise rates (adverse selection and moral hazard), and loan terms embed risk premia reflecting the information available about the borrower.
(b) Stiglitz, J. E., & Weiss, A. (1981). "Credit Rationing in Markets with Imperfect Information." *American Economic Review*, 71(3), 393–410.
(c) The economics of the seed's claim that the committed exposure [the seed's "loan" figure] is *sized* by the predictive attestation and that demonstrated calibration collapses the risk premia every other party in the chain must charge. Use for the sizing proposition (P10) rather than for the architecture itself.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-20. Fiduciary law: entrustment and service.**
(a) A fiduciary relationship arises where one party entrusts resources or discretion to another who undertakes to serve the entrustor's interest; the law constrains the fiduciary's pursuit of self-interest within that service.
(b) Frankel, T. (1983). "Fiduciary Law." *California Law Review*, 71, 795–. (Later expanded as *Fiduciary Law*, Oxford University Press, 2011.)
(c) The legal-formal home of the seed's doctrine that the aim of reflection is set by responsibility: the designated beneficiary of reflection can be the reflector, other persons, or the future state of a system — entrustment fixes which. Also the natural vocabulary for executor obligation owed to parties the executor will never meet.
(d) VERIFIED (citation corroborated via multiple secondary sources; first page 795 confirmed in search).

**RP-21. Fiduciary obligation beyond metaphor.**
(a) Fiduciary obligation analysed not as a relation of sentiment but as a constraint on the discretion of an entrusted party — enforceable conduct rules, not attitudes.
(b) DeMott, D. A. (1988). "Beyond Metaphor: An Analysis of Fiduciary Obligation." *Duke Law Journal*, 1988(5), 879–924.
(c) Doubly useful: it supplies the literal analysis of the seed's desireless-executor condition (the entrusted party's discretion is bounded by the terms of entrustment), and its title states the paper's own method — obligation stated as verifiable constraint rather than figure. A methodological citation as much as a substantive one.
(d) VERIFIED.

## Cluster D — Information partitioning, common knowledge, and modularity

**RP-22. Information partitions and common knowledge.**
(a) Each agent's knowledge is modelled as a partition over states of the world; an event is common knowledge when it holds in every state any agent's partition can reach — the formal basis for reasoning about who can be assumed to know what.
(b) Aumann, R. J. (1976). "Agreeing to Disagree." *The Annals of Statistics*, 4(6), 1236–1239.
(c) The formal vocabulary for the seed's public registry: certification published at a public address creates shared — indeed common — knowledge of the executor's standing, which is what lets a stranger's reliance ride on it without pairwise inspection.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-23. The use of knowledge in society.**
(a) The knowledge required for coordination is dispersed and locally held; institutional mechanisms that let each party act on partial knowledge outperform mechanisms requiring its aggregation.
(b) Hayek, F. A. (1945). "The Use of Knowledge in Society." *American Economic Review*, 35(4), 519–530.
(c) The canonical statement of the seed's "ignorance is load-bearing": each party's not-knowing is part of the design, because interface-level knowledge suffices where verification of interfaces is available. The paper's Proposition P8 should cite this alongside RP-24.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-24. Information hiding and module boundaries.**
(a) Decompose systems so that each module hides a design decision likely to change behind a stable interface; modules communicate through the interface, and interior decisions become other modules' non-knowledge by design.
(b) Parnas, D. L. (1972). "On the Criteria To Be Used in Decomposing Systems into Modules." *Communications of the ACM*, 15(12), 1053–1058.
(c) The engineering formalisation of the seed's sovereign-interior claim [the seed's "cell whose interior is sovereign and only its piping public" → implementation detail hidden behind a public interface] and of diligence collapsing to interface checks: the desiring party never needs the interior specification [the seed's "specs inside the wall"], only the certified interface.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-25. Design rules and the economics of modularity.**
(a) Modular architectures separate visible design rules (stable, public) from hidden modules (changeable, private); the separation enables independent experimentation without breaking interoperation.
(b) Baldwin, C. Y., & Clark, K. B. (2000). *Design Rules, Volume 1: The Power of Modularity*. MIT Press.
(c) Maps the standing specification [the seed's "code"] to visible design rules and execution to hidden modules; certification applies to interface conformance, which is exactly why interior ignorance is efficient rather than negligent.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-26. Design by contract (interface pre- and post-conditions).**
(a) Module interfaces are specified as obligations: pre-conditions the caller must satisfy, post-conditions the callee guarantees; conformance is checkable mechanically against the declared contract.
(b) Meyer, B. (1997). *Object-Oriented Software Construction* (2nd ed.). Prentice Hall. (First edition 1988.)
(c) The software-engineering form of the seed's specification-as-test: the pre-registered specification is the contract; post-engagement verification is a post-condition check; every violated post-condition is a finding, every satisfied one is silence. Cite when defining the specification (D10) and Proposition P5.
(d) UNVERIFIED (from memory; canonical, but check edition and pages before citing).

## Cluster E — Trust transitivity, demarcation, attestation chains

**RP-27. Least privilege and complete mediation.**
(a) Every user and program should operate with the minimum privileges required for its function, and every access to a protected resource must be checked — security principles stated as system design rules.
(b) Saltzer, J. H., & Schroeder, M. D. (1975). "The Protection of Information in Computer Systems." *Proceedings of the IEEE*, 63(9), 209–229.
(c) Formal ground for the seed's demarcation doctrine, including its sharpest clause: invocation authority [the seed's "keys"] is held by the specifying-and-certifying party, never domiciled with the executing party. Least privilege is the negative statement of the desireless-executor condition.
(d) VERIFIED (title, authors, venue, year confirmed; page range 209–229 from memory — confirm pages).

**RP-28. Capability-based authority.**
(a) Authority is held as unforgeable, communicable references to objects (capabilities) rather than as ambient identity-based permission; possession of a capability *is* the right to invoke it, and nothing else grants access.
(b) Dennis, J. B., & Van Horn, E. C. (1966). "Programming Semantics for Multiprogrammed Computations." *Communications of the ACM*, 9(3), 143–155.
(c) The operational form of the seed's demarcation: authority to invoke external resources circulates as inspectable tokens, which is what makes "where the certification lies" a checkable question. Also underwrites the two-kind distinction — a fixed-function resource is invoked by capability from within a compiled procedure.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-29. Object capabilities and least authority by construction.**
(a) In a pure object-capability system, components interact only through references they have been given; authority confinement and safe composition follow from language structure rather than external policy — including the analysis of the confused deputy (a competent agent misled into misusing authority it holds on another's behalf).
(b) Miller, M. S. (2006). *Robust Composition: Towards a Unified Approach to Access Control and Concurrency Control*. PhD thesis, Johns Hopkins University.
(c) Two mappings: (i) authority minimisation by construction — the executing party receives only the capabilities its contract requires; (ii) the confused deputy names the failure in which an executor serving a party's desire spends authority beyond the standing specification. The deputy analysis belongs in the failure-modes section.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-30. Trust management as explicit policy.**
(a) Decentralised systems encode trust as policy over credentials checked at access time, rather than as implicit identity hierarchy; authorisation becomes the evaluation of a proof.
(b) Blaze, M., Feigenbaum, J., & Lacy, J. (1996). "Decentralized Trust Management." *Proceedings of the 1996 IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy*, 164–173.
(c) The computational form of "trust that travels": reliance decisions are evaluations of presented credentials against policy, independent of acquaintance. This is the mechanics behind the seed's claim that diligence collapses to locating the certification's public address.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-31. Trust transitivity semantics.**
(a) Trust is not generally transitive; it propagates along chains only under constrained semantics (trust in a party's *recommendations or certifications*, not in its arbitrary judgements), and the constraints must be stated or the derived trust is unsound.
(b) Jøsang, A., & Pope, S. (2005). "Semantic Constraints for Trust Transitivity." *Proceedings of the Second Asia-Pacific Conference on Conceptual Modelling (APCCM 2005)*, 59–68. Australian Computer Society.
(c) Supplies the precise scope condition for the paper's transitivity proposition (P6): the chain transmits *certification*, not affection or general reliability; the paper's claim is exactly the restricted form this literature licenses, and should be stated as such.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-32. Certificate chains and path validation.**
(a) A public-key certificate binds an identity to a key under a superior certificate authority's signature; reliance proceeds by validating a chain of certificates back to a trusted anchor, each link vouching for the next.
(b) Cooper, D., et al. (2008). "Internet X.509 Public Key Infrastructure Certificate and Certificate Revocation List (CRL) Profile." IETF RFC 5280 (May 2008).
(c) The deployed, literal instance of the seed's chain of custody for trust: each link's standing underwriting the next, terminal reliance bounded by the weakest link, and revocation = the lapsed link that devalues every downstream reliance without reducing anyone's obligation.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-33. Remote attestation architecture (three-role evidence model).**
(a) Standardises attestation as a three-role flow: an attester produces signed evidence about its environment; a verifier appraises the evidence against policy and reference values; a relying party consumes the resulting attestation result to make its decision.
(b) Birkel, R., Wheeler, D. W., Panwei, L., & Eckel, M. (2023). "Remote ATtestation procedureS (RATS) Architecture." IETF RFC 9334.
(c) The closest deployed formalisation of the seed's whole three-party trust architecture: executing party = attester; specifying-and-certifying party = verifier (with the standing specification as appraisal policy); desiring party = relying party. The paper should present this as convergent prior art for the *structure*, with the predictive attestation as the element the standard lacks.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-34. Conformity assessment and accreditation (institutional layer).**
(a) Standards family governing third-party assurance: certification bodies are themselves accredited against meta-standards (competence, impartiality, consistency), and independent inspection bodies are graded by independence type; the registry of accredited bodies is public.
(b) ISO/IEC 17021-1:2015, *Conformity assessment — Requirements for certification bodies auditing and certifying management systems*; ISO/IEC 17020:2012, *Conformity assessment — Requirements for the operation of various types of bodies performing inspection*.
(c) The institutional literalisation of the seed's building-department figure [→ the specifying-and-certifying party as an accredited registry institution]: certification has a public address, certifiers are themselves certified (the chain recursing upward), and the boundary-verification function is a distinct, independence-typed role.
(d) VERIFIED (both standards confirmed via search).

**RP-35. Chain of custody for evidence.**
(a) Evidence must be accompanied by a documented, unbroken record of collection, transfer, and storage; a gap in the record devalues the evidence regardless of its content.
(b) Kent, K., Chevalier, S., Grance, T., & Dang, H. (2006). *Guide to Integrating Forensic Techniques into Incident Response*. NIST Special Publication 800-86. National Institute of Standards and Technology.
(c) Direct formalisation of the seed's receipt discipline: every relayed claim must travel with its attestation of provenance, and "a claim relayed without its receipt is a certificate nobody checked" is the custody-gap rule applied to trust rather than to exhibits.
(d) VERIFIED.

## Cluster F — The estimate as predictive attestation (mandatory element)

Casey, in the seed's words: **"The most important job for a contractor is the estimate. If they get known for nailing estimates, that is a different kind of decision model."**

Literal treatment to be developed by the paper: a **predictive attestation** is a quantified forecast of scope, cost, and duration, issued by the executing party before engagement, under uncertainty. It differs from state-evaluating decision models in two formal respects: (i) its object is a *future* state, not a present one; (ii) it **collateralises the issuer's standing on the realised outcome** — the forecast converts the issuer's future performance into a posted bond denominated in track record. Calibration history (forecast against realised outcome, across repeated engagements) then functions as a *renewable component of certification*, and the relying parties' committed exposure is *sized* by the forecast — making estimation accuracy the load-bearing quantity of the trust chain. Its formal homes:

**RP-36. Proper scoring rules.**
(a) A scoring rule assigns a forecast a score as a function of the realised outcome; the rule is strictly proper when truthful reporting of one's probability beliefs uniquely maximises expected score — honesty becomes the dominant strategy.
(b) Brier, G. W. (1950). "Verification of forecasts expressed in terms of probability." *Monthly Weather Review*, 78(1), 1–3. Synthesis: Gneiting, T., & Raftery, A. E. (2007). "Strictly Proper Scoring Rules, Prediction, and Estimation." *Journal of the American Statistical Association*, 102(477), 359–378.
(c) Two load-bearing uses: (i) scoring rules make the forecast–realisation delta a well-defined, non-gameable measurement — the backward-looking receipt of forecasting ability; (ii) propriety guarantees that the executor's dominant strategy is honest estimation, which is what lets the chain rely on it. This is the mathematics of "known for nailing estimates."
(d) VERIFIED (both).

**RP-37. Calibration and forecast verification (reliability measurement).**
(a) Calibration (reliability) is the statistical correspondence between stated forecast probabilities and observed relative frequencies across a forecaster's repeated forecasts, measurable via reliability diagrams and decompositions of the probability score.
(b) Murphy, A. H., & Winkler, R. L. (1977). "Reliability of Subjective Probability Forecasts of Precipitation and Temperature." *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, Series C (Applied Statistics)*, 26(1), 41–47. **Correction found during verification:** often miscited to *JASA*; the venue is *JRSS-C (Applied Statistics)*.
(c) The measurement procedure behind "calibration history as renewable certification": across repeated engagements, plot stated against realised; the resulting statistic is the component of standing that the predictive attestation renews or degrades.
(d) VERIFIED (with venue correction).

**RP-38. Measured track records of expert forecasters.**
(a) Long-run scoring of expert forecasters shows large, persistent, measurable individual differences in calibration; expertise and confidence do not predict accuracy, but recorded track record does.
(b) Tetlock, P. E. (2005). *Expert Political Judgment: How Good Is It? How Can We Know?* Princeton University Press.
(c) Empirical warrant for the paper's claim that a *known-for-calibration* executor is a real, detectable, economically distinct category — the foundation of the "different kind of decision model" claim: reliance on measured track record rather than on credential or confidence.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-39. Base rates and the inside/outside view.**
(a) Intuitive prediction over-weights case-specific evidence and neglects prior base rates; normatively, prediction should anchor on the reference class and adjust by case specifics.
(b) Kahneman, D., & Tversky, A. (1973). "On the Psychology of Prediction." *Psychological Review*, 80(4), 237–251.
(c) The formal description of the procedure the seed attributes to its estimator: "base rates induced from every past job, re-weighted by a reading of this one" — reference-class anchor, case-specific re-weighting. Cite when defining how a predictive attestation should be constructed.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-40. Reference-class forecasting for engagements.**
(a) Forecasts of cost and duration should be derived from the distribution of *realised outcomes* of a class of comparable past engagements, corrected for case specifics, precisely because bottom-up internal estimates are systematically biased (optimism bias, strategic misrepresentation).
(b) Flyvbjerg, B. (2006). "From Nobel Prize to Project Management: Getting Risks Right." *Project Management Journal*, 37(3), 5–15. **Correction found during verification:** often miscited to the *International Journal of Project Management*; the venue is *Project Management Journal*.
(c) The applied methodology for issuing predictive attestations in the engagement domain: the executor's forecast is disciplined by the realised-outcome distribution of comparable engagements, not by introspection alone. Also the standard citation for why *un*anchored estimates are untrustworthy — the failure mode the calibration record exists to catch.
(d) VERIFIED (with venue correction).

**RP-41. Surety and performance bonding.**
(a) A surety bond is a third-party guarantee that an obligation will be performed: the surety (having underwritten the principal's competence) pays the obligee on default; bonding practice prices the principal's track record, and bonding capacity itself functions as certifiable standing.
(b) Russell, J. S. (2000). *Surety Bonds for Construction Contracts*. ASCE Press.
(c) The institutional home of "collateralises the issuer's standing on the outcome": financial third parties convert demonstrated performance history into a priced, renewable guarantee. The paper's predictive attestation is this institution with the scoring rule made explicit and the track record itself as the bond.
(d) VERIFIED.

## Cluster G — Obligations denominated against the forecast: burn-down and non-recoverable time (mandatory addendum)

Casey, 2026-10-27: **"Every other link in the chain is a debt that the contractor is now trying to pay on time."**

Literal treatment: once the forecast is issued and relied upon, each subsequent obligation in the chain is denominated against the forecast; execution becomes a schedule of discharges; and late discharge is cost paid in time — on the activities with no schedule slack, elapsed time is the one input that cannot be borrowed, re-ordered, or substituted. The conformance test this implies is not only terminal conformance to specification but *continuous* measurement of realised-against-forecast burn-down, with the variance surfaced while corrective action is still cheap. Formal homes:

**RP-42. Earned value management (EVM).**
(a) Progress is measured continuously against a pre-established baseline: planned value (what should be done by now), earned value (what is done), actual cost (what it took); variances and performance indices are computed mid-execution, and estimate-at-completion is a running revision of the terminal forecast.
(b) Fleming, Q. W., & Koppelman, J. M. (2010). *Earned Value Project Management* (4th ed.). Project Management Institute.
(c) The deployed instrument of the addendum's conformance test: realised-against-forecast burn-down measured on a fixed cadence, with the delta surfaced *during* execution rather than at completion. EVM's estimate-at-completion is precisely the predictive attestation being revised in public as the engagement proceeds.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-43. Schedule-risk analysis.**
(a) Activity durations are treated as random variables; Monte Carlo analysis of the activity network (including path-merging effects) yields a distribution of completion dates against which realised progress is monitored.
(b) Hulett, D. T. (2009). *Practical Schedule Risk Analysis*. Gower.
(c) Formalises the forecast as a distribution rather than a point, and supplies the monitoring machinery for mid-execution detection that the terminal date is drifting — the quantitative form of "surfaced while correction remains cheap."
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-44. The critical-path method (non-substitutable time).**
(a) In an activity network, the longest path fixes the minimum completion time; activities on that path have zero slack — any delay there extends the whole engagement, while delay within slack does not.
(b) Kelley, J. E., & Walker, M. R. (1959). "Critical-Path Planning and Scheduling." *Proceedings of the Eastern Joint Computer Conference*, 160–173.
(c) The exact formal content of "time — the one non-borrowable currency": slack is borrowable time, critical-path time is not. The paper's debt literalisation (late discharge as cost accruing in time) should be stated *as* monotone accumulation of irrecoverable delay on zero-slack obligations, not as sentiment about clocks.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-45. PERT (stochastic duration baselines).**
(a) Planning technique in which uncertain activity durations are given distributions and combined to estimate a completion-date distribution for the whole programme, used to monitor programme risk.
(b) Malcolm, D. G., Roseboom, J. H., Clark, C. E., & Fazar, W. (1959). "Application of a Technique for Research and Development Program Evaluation." *Operations Research*, 7(5), 646–669.
(c) The historical origin of forecast-based execution monitoring, and the standard citation that engagement forecasting is probabilistic at issue time. Cite alongside RP-43/44 when defining the forecast baseline against which burn-down is measured.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-46. Statistical process control.**
(a) Process output is monitored on control charts that separate common-cause from special-cause variation; drift is detected and corrected early, because defect detection at the end of the line is the most expensive place to find it.
(b) Shewhart, W. A. (1931). *Economic Control of Quality of Manufactured Product*. D. Van Nostrand.
(c) The quality-engineering argument for continuous over terminal conformance checking: the cost of correction rises with the time a deviation goes undetected. Formal warrant for the paper's Proposition P12 (continuous conformance), independent of the scheduling literature.
(d) VERIFIED.

**RP-47. Liquidated damages and the pricing of delay.**
(a) Parties pre-specify the compensation payable for late performance; a well-formed liquidated-damages clause prices expected harm from delay at contracting time, making delay a pre-priced rather than litigated cost.
(b) Goetz, C. J., & Scott, R. E. (1977). "Liquidated Damages, Penalties and the Just Compensation Principle: Some Notes on an Enforcement Model and a Theory of Efficient Breach." *Columbia Law Review*, 77(4), 554–.
(c) The contract-law form of the addendum's debt claim: once the forecast is relied upon, late discharge carries a price fixed in advance, and the clause is itself a predictive attestation of duration by the drafter. Connects the burn-down doctrine to enforceable obligation.
(d) VERIFIED (title, authors, venue, year, first page confirmed; full page range from memory — confirm).

## Gaps, corrections, and cautions for the paper

1. **No single prior art for "System 3."** The seed's third system — external, *mediation-gated*, latency-indifferent between engagements — has no canonical source. Nearest neighbours are RP-1 (external states), RP-2/RP-3 (system-level attribution of cognition), RP-6 (delegation depth), and RP-9 (an *internal* tripartition, which the paper must distinguish itself from). The contribution claim should be framed as exactly this gap: a third system defined by locus and mediation rather than by speed.
2. **"Predictive attestation" is a coinage.** The composition (scoring-rule-verified forecast + collateralised standing + exposure sizing) has no single literature; its components are RP-36 to RP-41, with RP-19 for sizing. Present it as a definition built from verified parts, never as a reference to an existing term of art.
3. **Two venue corrections locked in during verification:** Flyvbjerg (2006) is in *Project Management Journal* 37(3), 5–15 (RP-40); Murphy & Winkler (1977) is in *JRSS-C (Applied Statistics)* 26(1), 41–47 (RP-37). Do not propagate the common misattributions.
4. **Unverified items remaining:** Kahneman (2011) synthesis (RP-7); Meyer (1997) design by contract (RP-26); page ranges for Saltzer & Schroeder (RP-27) and Goetz & Scott (RP-47). Re-check before submission; the argument does not depend on them.
5. **Contested ground to state honestly:** trust transitivity is contested (RP-31) — the paper's claim must be the restricted certification-transmission form; certification intermediaries can fail by design (RP-16) — a limitation, not a footnote; the desireless executor is an idealisation, and learning executors acquire endogenous dispositions that approximate preferences — the paper should say so.
6. **Deferred as redundant:** Williamson-style transaction-cost economics (covered functionally by RP-13/RP-18); stewardship theory (covered by RP-20/RP-21); runtime-verification literature (covered functionally by RP-26 plus the EVM cluster); ISO/IEC 17065 (same family as RP-34, not separately verified).
7. **Standard cluster note.** ISO/IEC and NIST documents (RP-34, RP-35) are normative standards rather than peer-reviewed literature; cite them as convergent institutional practice, not as theoretical warrant — the theoretical warrant comes from the economics and security clusters.
