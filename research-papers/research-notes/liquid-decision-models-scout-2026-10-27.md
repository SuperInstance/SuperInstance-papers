# Liquid decision models — a scouting note

**Proposal under survey** (Casey, 2026-10-27): derive a *decision* model from the *liquid* model family, the way decision models have been derived from generative ones — a System 1 layer that is pre-linguistic, weighted and fast, projecting "what should come next" (answer from script / pull a named file / escalate), fetching and issuing only through a governed aperture (the pincher), with every pull provenance-bearing, and improving continually, on a quilt substrate of cells and an active ledger.

**Headline verdict.** The derivation recipe is proven and cheap (§1). The liquid substrate is real, tiny and unusually well matched to irregular event streams (§2). But the specific intersection — liquid networks *as* judges, routers or agent decision layers — is **essentially empty in the published record** (§3). Nothing in the liquid literature supplies the two things the proposal needs most: provenance (a property of the harness, not the network) and online improvement (liquid weights are frozen after training; only the dynamics adapt). The proposal is therefore not a literature application but a construction: a proven recipe (§1) pointed at a promising substrate (§2), with the hard part — *governed, attested updating* — still unbuilt by anyone.

**Process.** Sources: the primary papers (LTC, Hasani et al., AAAI 2021; CfC, *Nature Machine Intelligence* 2022; NCP, *NMI* 2020; robust flight, *Science Robotics* 2023), the `ncps` repository read directly, the RLHF/judge/router literature (InstructGPT; JudgeLM; PandaLM; Prometheus; RouteLLM; a 2026 probe-judge paper read in full text), and JEPA planning work (V-JEPA 2-AC; JEPA-TTT). Claims below are marked verified (read in a primary or near-primary source) or reported (secondary). Two popular claims failed verification and are corrected in §2.

## 1. The derivation move: decision models from non-decision models

Four variants are in production use, and they share one skeleton.

**(a) Head-swap on the generative model itself.** The RLHF reward model *is* a decision model derived from a generative one: take the supervised-fine-tuned LM, remove the language-modelling head, fit a single scalar head, train on pairwise preferences under a Bradley–Terry loss (verified). InstructGPT's 6B reward model steered a 175B policy from ~33k ranked prompts. Cost driver: the labels, not the compute — one university course's accounting puts pairwise-feedback annotation at roughly a sixth of demonstration annotation for the same exercise.

**(b) Probes on a frozen backbone.** Freeze the big model; train only a linear or small MLP readout on its hidden states. A 2026 paper on rubric judging froze Qwen3-1.7B, trained a linear classifier on GPT-4o criterion labels, and the resulting probe judge *beat* an 8B generative judge as an RL reward (policy score 0.643 vs 0.594) at 10.7× less judge time (verified in the paper text). The decision was already in the representation; generation was the expensive way to read it out.

**(c) Distillation of a prompted judge into a small fine-tuned judge.** JudgeLM (GPT-4 judgements → Vicuna 7B/13B/33B; >90% agreement with its teacher), PandaLM (GPT-3.5 → LLaMA-7B), Prometheus (rubric scoring) (verified). The honest caveat, from a cross-evaluation study: fine-tuned judges are **scheme-bound** — JudgeLM-7B scores 82.4% on its own test and 48.7% on MT-Bench. Distilled deciders inherit their teacher's *format* of judgement, not judgement in general.

**(d) Predict-then-score (the JEPA shape).** Freeze the encoder; train a comparatively small action-conditioned predictor; at decision time, roll each candidate forward in latent space and read the score off the *predicted* state (V-JEPA 2-AC: frozen encoder, a 300M predictor trained on under 62 hours of robot data, model-predictive control scoring candidates by distance to a goal embedding — Meta-reported). This variant matches Casey's wording most closely: the decider *projects what comes next* and judges the projection. Its continual-learning extension also exists: JEPA-TTT updates **only the predictor** at test time, from self-supervised prediction error, while encoder and reward head stay frozen — improving planning on all eight dynamics shifts tested.

**The reusable recipe.** Freeze the expensive non-decision model. Harvest its judgements (or its representations, or its predicted futures) as supervision *over the exact situations the decider will face*. Train a small artefact whose output type is a decision — scalar, class, or route. Validate on a *different* scheme from the training scheme, because (c) shows that is where distilled deciders die. Budgets in the literature: 10⁴–10⁵ labelled situations for heads and probes; hours-to-days of teacher inference, not weeks.

## 2. Liquid models: state of the art

**The mechanism, precisely.** An LTC neuron obeys dx/dt = −[1/τ + f(x, I, θ)]·x + f(x, I, θ)·A, where f is a small learned network. The *effective* time constant, τ_sys = τ/(1 + τf), is therefore a function of the current state and input: a neuron can integrate slowly through calm stretches and react sharply to change, and both state and τ_sys are provably bounded, so the dynamics cannot blow up (verified). **The weights θ do not update at inference.** Two widespread claims — that liquid networks "rewire themselves" or "keep learning after training" — are imprecise to the point of error: what adapts online is the *dynamics* (state and effective timescales); the knowledge in the weights is as frozen as any transformer's. The reputable one-line summary: "Training fixes their weights as in any network; the liquid part is the dynamics, which the input reshapes at every step." Any continual improvement in Casey's sense must be bolted on — an outer re-distillation loop, or a JEPA-TTT-style small updateable component.

**Sizes.** This family is *minute*. The canonical NCP driving policy: **19 control neurons, 253 synapses** (verified). Drone and parking policies are reported at 12–30 neurons. CfC models on standard time-series benchmarks sit in the 10⁴–10⁵ parameter range, and CfC's closed form removes the ODE solver entirely — training and inference run **one to five orders of magnitude faster** than solver-based counterparts (paper abstract, verified); on the UCI activity-recognition benchmark the best CfC variant beats the Latent-ODE state of the art (87.0% vs 84.6%) at roughly 1/80th of its per-epoch training time.

**Deployments.** The flagship is *Science Robotics* 2023: liquid flight-navigation agents, trained by imitation in one environment, flew to target in drastically shifted ones (season, scenery, noise, occlusion) where comparison architectures failed — the robustness is attributed to the input-dependent dynamics discarding irrelevant features (verified). Medical and industrial time-series work is plentiful. **Microcontroller evidence is thin**: suitability for microcontrollers is widely argued from size and the ECG literature repeats it, but this survey found **no primary-source demonstration of an LTC/CfC running on ESP32-class hardware** — a searched-for, not-found item. On Jetson-class hardware nothing in the architecture is a strain; the models are smaller than the sensor drivers.

**Tooling and maintenance.** `ncps` (Lechner) is the reference library: PyTorch and TensorFlow/Keras, LTC and CfC cells, NCP/AutoNCP wirings, **Apache-2.0**, ~2,400 GitHub stars, not archived, maintained by the original author (verified at the repository). Liquid AI (the founders' company) is the commercial centre of gravity, but note the naming trap, because the fleet already lives in it: **LFM2/LFM2.5 — the "liquid" models resident on the Thor — are not LTC/CfC networks.** They are a separate architecture (gated short convolutions plus a few grouped-query attention layers) sharing the brand and the efficiency philosophy, not the liquid dynamics. Nothing about running LFM2.5 makes the fleet's System 1 "liquid" in the sense of this survey.

## 3. The intersection: liquid networks as judges, routers, gates

Searched directly ("liquid neural network" × judge / router / gating / tool selection / agent), plus the adjacent mesh-intelligence and MoE literatures. Findings:

- **As a judge or scorer of other models' outputs: nothing found.** No paper, library or product surfaced.
- **As an LLM router or tool selector: nothing found.** The routing-decision literature (RouteLLM and successors) uses matrix factorisation, BERT-class classifiers and small LMs — never liquid cells.
- **As a gating signal: one tangential instance.** A gesture-to-music system feeds an LTC hidden state into a mixture-of-experts gating network — liquid state *informing* a gate, in a creative system, documented on a project page rather than in a peer-reviewed venue (weak source, real instance).
- **As a forecasting front-end to routing: one instance.** A network-provisioning framework uses a liquid module for demand forecasting whose outputs feed a (non-neural) routing optimiser — liquid *upstream* of decisions, not making them.
- **Closest in spirit: a 2026 preprint on mesh intelligence** argues formally that a distributed, asynchronously-observed substrate *must* have an input-dependent timescale and elapsed-gap-dependent updates — the two defining liquid properties — and deploys an MIT-family liquid cell in the *reservoir* stance: fixed substrate, trained readouts. That is recognisably Casey's shape (an always-on liquid layer under governed readouts) arrived at independently, for mesh timing rather than for agent decisions.

**Verdict: the intersection is empty where it matters.** Nobody has published a liquid judge, a liquid router, or a liquid decision layer in an agent stack. That is a genuine gap rather than a warning — the neighbouring fields each hold half the idea — but it means the fleet would be building, not adopting, and should budget accordingly.

## 4. Fit and friction, requirement by requirement

| Casey's requirement | Liquid route | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-linguistic weighted decisions | **Natural fit** | Continuous state, no tokens anywhere; the dynamics *are* weightings evolving in time. This is the family's home turf. |
| Projects "what should come next" | **Natural fit** | Continuous-time state is a running projection; readout heads turn it into next-action scores (cf. recipe (d)). |
| Routing as output (script / pull / escalate) | **Buildable** | A readout over discrete pull-targets is an ordinary classifier head (AutoNCP wirings give structured outputs); RouteLLM proves the *pattern*, albeit with other substrates. |
| Governed aperture, provenance-bearing pulls | **Fights the substrate** | A network cannot attest anything. It can *select* a pull; only the pincher harness around it can execute, bound and *log* it. Provenance must be a property of the channel, with the model emitting an auditable decision record (inputs hash, state summary, chosen target, margin) for the harness to countersign. |
| Continual improvement | **Fights the substrate** | Weights are frozen by design (§2). Improvement needs an outer loop — scheduled re-distillation from new ledger outcomes — or a small updateable component in the JEPA-TTT pattern. Neither ships with `ncps`. |
| Tiny-hardware fit | **Natural fit (Jetson), unproven (ESP32)** | 10⁴–10⁵ parameters and no solver (CfC) is nothing to a Jetson; ESP32 deployment is argued, not demonstrated (§2). |
| Quilt / ledger compatibility | **Buildable** | Liquid cells are native to irregular event streams — elapsed time between events is structure, not noise, which suits ledger traffic exactly. The work is featurisation: ledger events are symbolic; the cell eats numeric vectors plus Δt. Somebody must design that encoding, and it will decide what the decider can perceive. |

**The hardest open problem** the survey surfaces: *attested updating*. Casey's loop needs the decider to improve from the outcomes of its own past decisions — but a routing decision's outcome label ("the pull was right", "the escalation was wasted") arrives late, noisily, and through the very channel the decider governs. Liquid dynamics give adaptation of behaviour with frozen knowledge; the distillation recipes give knowledge updates that are offline and scheme-bound. Nobody has joined them under a provenance regime — where every weight update is itself a claim that must carry its evidence (which episodes, labelled by whom, verified how) before the pincher admits it. Until that joint exists, "a System 1 that keeps getting better" is a retraining schedule with good branding — which may be enough, but should be chosen knowingly.

## 5. The specified division of labour, assessed

Casey's proof-of-concept split (2026-10-27): **Thor = data foundry** (inference, gathering, simulation in parallel), **ProArt RTX 4050 = training bench** for the tiny models in bursts, **Orin = deployment target** at the sensor.

**Training placement: the survey supports the ProArt, and the reason is stack risk, not speed.** A CfC/NCP training run is trivially small — minutes on a CPU, seconds-to-minutes on any GPU; both boxes are absurdly over-provisioned, and the workload's real shape is *many short runs* (wiring and hyperparameter search over AutoNCP configurations), i.e. exactly a burst pattern on a provisioned, cooled laptop. The deciding evidence is environmental: the Thor's PyTorch is a carefully pinned SBSA-wheel build (a specific Jetson-stage torch with companion-library pins and registered library paths) that simultaneously serves the fleet's resident endpoints — every experimental install there risks the foundry itself. The ProArt's WSL stack is mainstream x86 CUDA/PyTorch, where `pip install ncps` is a one-line, disposable act. Verdict: **train on the 4050; keep the Thor's environment frozen for generation.** The calculus flips only if training ever grows past tiny (e.g. Liquid-S4-scale sequence models), which this proposal does not need.

**What the Thor foundry must emit.** The tiny model learns decisions over *event streams*, so the foundry's product is episodes, not documents. Specification:

- **Format:** JSONL episode files (Parquet once volume justifies it). Each episode: a time-ordered step list; each step carries Δt since the previous event (**never resample the gaps away — elapsed time is the liquid cell's native input**), a compact numeric feature vector (a small embedding of the situation — the Thor's resident MiniLM/JEV-rack features are the obvious source — plus scalar state: queue depths, latencies, link/battery state), the candidate action set, the teacher's chosen action, and an **outcome label** (the choice's verified downstream result and its cost), without which the student learns imitation, not consequence.
- **Provenance per record:** which resident model or judge produced the label, which source stream or file the situation came from, and the foundry run id — so any pull the trained model later makes can be audited back to the records that taught it.
- **Mixture:** shard generation by scenario family — normal traffic, drift (timing shifts, sensor dropout), and failure injection — because the liquid claim being bought is robustness under shift; a student trained only on calm traffic tests nothing.
- **Volume:** the §1 recipes trained deciders on 10⁴–10⁵ labelled situations. A 10⁴–10⁵-parameter CfC needs far less to show a first signal — low thousands of episodes — and the full band (10⁴–10⁵ episodes) is an hours-scale generation job for the Thor's parallel resident models, not a programme. *(Volume-to-quality mapping here is reasoned from the recipe literature, not measured; flagged as the experiment's job to check.)*

**Deployment leg:** the trained CfC exports as a few tens of KB of weights and a small runtime; on the Orin it runs as a plain PyTorch (or hand-ported) forward pass at the sensor, inside the pincher harness that executes and logs its pulls.

## 6. Smallest first experiment — PROPOSED, not run

**Question.** Does a liquid decision layer earn its place — i.e., does a CfC router beat cheap non-liquid deciders *specifically under timing drift*, the condition the whole family exists for?

**Setup, all hardware in-house.** Pick the one routing decision the fleet makes constantly, in simulated form: an incoming event is routed to *answer-from-script*, *pull a named source*, or *escalate*. Thor generates ~20,000 labelled decision episodes over a synthetic stream with scripted calm/drift/failure phases (labels from a resident big model plus the JEV judge, outcomes verified against the simulation's ground truth). ProArt trains three students on identical data: (i) a CfC with AutoNCP wiring (target ≤10⁵ parameters, via `ncps`); (ii) a small MLP probe on the same features; (iii) a small GRU. Orin deploys all three and replays a held-out stream with *injected* drift — shifted inter-event timing, dropped sensors, reordered bursts.

**Read-outs, decided in advance.** Route accuracy calm vs drifted, per model; decision latency on the Orin; and the provenance check — every routed decision in the replay must carry a complete harness record. **Success criterion:** the CfC must beat the MLP probe under drift by a margin worth its novelty (say ≥5 points of route accuracy), not merely match it in calm conditions. If it cannot win on its home turf, the liquid route is falsified cheaply and the fleet keeps the probe — which is itself a fine System 1. Estimated cost: days of agent time, hours of machine time, nothing bought.
